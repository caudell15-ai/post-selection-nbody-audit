# v1.6 Scientific Freeze

This release accompanies the manuscript **From Exploratory Perturber Screening to Post-Selection N-body Audit: A Reproducibility Case Study Beyond Neptune**.

## Scope

This is a numerical-methods and reproducibility case study. It does **not** claim detection of an undiscovered planet or observational preference for Candidate #60.

## What is frozen in v1.6

- Candidate #60 is retained as the historical post-hoc focal case.
- The 1000-member fixed-bulk-orbit orientation null places #60 at the 88.6th empirical percentile.
- Complete 90-candidate replication places #60 8th by worst-object response and 6th by the Ammonite-specific response.
- Candidate #66 produces the largest common-REBOUND response in the recovered 90-candidate screen, but is not substituted as the focal case after inspection.
- WHFast timestep refinement, IAS15 cross-checks, deterministic PX-A mass and GM-precision tests, epoch-translation audit, full-screen replication, and sampling-cadence audit are complete within the stated scope.
- Phase 2B covariance clones and independent reimplementation are explicitly future work, not requirements for the claims made in this methods case study.

## Provenance status

The archive preserves the recovered 90-candidate population, bulk corridor bounds, hard constraints, heuristic score, frozen protocols, code, execution logs, SHA-256 hashes, and known environment information.

The surviving archive does not permit reliable reconstruction of the exact original angular-generation bounds/seed or whether the 90-candidate set was generated by a single pass or adaptive refinement. Those limits are disclosed rather than reconstructed from hindsight.

Ten of sixteen principal canonical artifacts are presently available as byte-identical files and verify against their recorded SHA-256 values. Six historical result CSVs are not presently available as their original byte-identical files; their expected hashes, serialization rules, and contemporaneous execution records are preserved. They are not reconstructed from rounded console output.

## Scientific freeze

Final external review consensus was unanimous that the scientific content could be frozen and that no additional experiment is required for the claims made in v1.6. Subsequent changes should be archival/citation corrections only unless an actual scientific error is discovered.
